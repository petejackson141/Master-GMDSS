What AIS is

AIS is an automatic tracking system on VHF. Every AIS-equipped ship keeps broadcasting who it is, where it is and where it is going, and receives the same from every ship around it. Nobody has to press anything or speak on the radio.

The ship's AIS display, chart plotter, ECDIS or radar overlay turns those messages into targets on the screen, so you can see a ship's name and course before you can even see it by eye. Its main job is collision avoidance and traffic monitoring by shore stations.

What each ship sends

Static

Set once when the AIS is installed.

  • MMSI
  • Vessel name
  • Call sign
  • Type and size

Dynamic

From the ship's GPS and compass, updated all the time.

  • GPS position
  • SOG (speed over ground)
  • COG (course over ground)
  • Heading
  • Navigation status

Voyage

Typed in by the crew for each trip.

  • Destination
  • ETA

How often it reports depends on what it's doing: every 2 to 10 seconds underway (faster when it's fast or turning), and every 3 minutes at anchor.

Reading the MMSI

Every AIS target has a 9-digit MMSI. The first digits tell you what kind of station it is.

Ship428 123 456Starts with the country code (MID). 428 = Israel
Coast station004 281 23400 + MID
SAR aircraft111 428 123111 + MID
Nav aid994 281 23499 + MID
AIS-SART970 721 234970, no MID
MOB device972 721 234972, no MID
AIS-EPIRB974 721 234974, no MID

Memory hook for the 97x devices: 0 = SART, 2 = MOB, 4 = EPIRB.

Distress devices on AIS

AIS-SARTs, MOB devices and AIS-EPIRBs use the same AIS channels to put survivors on every nearby ship's screen. They show as a circle with a cross and set off an alarm with a message like SART ACTIVE or EPIRB ACTIVE, plus their exact GPS position. In a self-test you see SART TEST or EPIRB TEST instead.

Don't rely on AIS alone

  • Not every vessel has AIS, and it can be switched off. Small boats, some fishing boats and warships may not show at all.
  • Positions come from the other ship's own GPS. If its GPS or settings are wrong, its target is wrong too.
  • Voyage data such as destination is typed by the crew and is often out of date.
  • Keep a proper lookout and use radar as well. AIS helps you avoid collisions; it doesn't replace the Colregs.
