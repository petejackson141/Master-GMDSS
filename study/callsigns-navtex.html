The basics
  • Call signs and identification allow the type of wireless station to be identified
  • The call sign consists of letters and numbers
  • The first two components identify the country: two letters, a letter and a number, or a number and a letter. Israel is 4Y or 4X

NAVTEX

Navigational Telex

Automatic maritime safety information (MSI) from coast stations

518 kHzEnglish, international
490 kHzlocal language, national
400 NMreception range
Every 4 hup to 10 min per station

What it is

  • International system for automatic transmission and reception of safety and weather messages (MSI) from coast stations worldwide
  • Introduced in Boston in 1983; part of GMDSS since 1999
  • Carries SAR, navigational and safety warnings, meteorological warnings, ice warnings, weather forecasts, special messages
  • After an EPIRB alert, the RCC may broadcast a distress message on NAVTEX to widen the search

Frequencies & range

  • 518 kHz: English, international
  • 490 kHz: local language, national
  • 4209.5 kHz: tropical zone, international
  • Received up to 400 NM; maximum range at night
  • Stations with the same ID letter are ~800 NM apart, so you never receive both
  • Stations time-share and transmit every 4 hours, up to 10 minutes each
Message header: ZCZC B1 B2 B3 B4 … NNNN
ZCZCstart
PB1: transmitting station (1 letter)
AB2: message type (1 letter)
13B3 B4: message number 01–99
NNNNend

Example: ZCZC PA13 · ROUTINE · 201908 UTC MAR 09 (date/time as ddHHmm) = Haifa Radio, navigational warning, number 13.

Areas & using it

  • 21 NAVAREAS: 16 + 5 Arctic areas added 01.07.2010; stations in each lettered A–Z
  • Before sailing, leave it on 4–12 hours to receive a full round of messages
  • Screen-only or printer models; printer alarms when paper runs out
  • Program the station letter and message type (A–Z) to skip unwanted messages
  • In Area A1 but outside NAVTEX coverage: need MSI on HF, Telex or INMARSAT
