        Key facts

        What it is
        An AIS-SART is a small AIS transmitter with its own GNSS (GPS) receiver. It sends its position as an AIS message, so the rescue ship sees it as a target on its AIS display, chart plotter, ECDIS or radar with AIS overlay.
        MMSI 970
        Its ID always starts with 970, then a 2-digit maker code and a 4-digit serial (for example 970 01 2345). 972 is an AIS man-overboard device, 974 an AIS-EPIRB.
        On the display
        A circle with a cross, and an alarm with the text SART ACTIVE. In a self-test it sends SART TEST instead.
        Frequencies
        The two AIS channels, 161.975 MHz (AIS 1) and 162.025 MHz (AIS 2), in the VHF band.
        How often
        Once a minute it sends a burst of 8 messages, 4 on each AIS channel, so the position keeps updating.
        Switching on
        Starts transmitting within about a minute. The GNSS light shows when it has a position fix.
        Range
        At least 5 nm from a ship, about 5–10 nm in practice with the SART about 1 m above the sea. Much further from an aircraft. Height matters: get it up on its pole.
        No radar needed
        Unlike a radar SART it does not wait to be triggered: it transmits by itself, and it gives an exact GNSS position instead of 12 dots.
        Battery
        At least 96 hours of operation. Replace by the expiry date on the label.
        Carriage
        Can be carried instead of a radar SART to meet the SOLAS SART requirement.
        Testing
        Use the TEST button, not the ON switch. Your own AIS should show SART TEST. Keep tests short.
        Switched on by mistake
        Switch it off straight away. Ships' AIS displays nearby will have shown a SART ACTIVE alarm.
