        Key facts

        406 MHz
        5 W digital burst about every 50 seconds. Carries the beacon's unique ID, and its GNSS position when it has a fix.
        121.5 MHz
        Low-power homing signal. Aircraft and ships use it to find the beacon in the final stage of the search.
        Strobe
        High-intensity white light flashing 20–30 times a minute to help searchers see the beacon at night.
        Battery
        At least 48 hours of operation. Replace by the expiry date on the label.
        HRU
        Hydrostatic release unit. Frees the EPIRB at 1.5–4 m depth so it floats up and activates. Usually replaced every 2 years.
        Category I / II
        Cat I is float-free and activates automatically. Cat II is manual only.
        Testing
        Self-test monthly, annual test, and shore-based maintenance at intervals of no more than 5 years.
        Registration
        Register the beacon with the national authority. The RCC uses it to identify the vessel and call its emergency contacts.
        False alert
        Switch the beacon off and immediately tell the nearest RCC or coast station. Never just switch off and say nothing.
